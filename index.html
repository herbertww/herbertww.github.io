CounterBank

Founder · live

The treasury of Synterra, the first organized nation for sentients.

CounterBank starts from a claim about scarcity. The ingredient running out is human attention. A GPU lying idle is unpriced compute; a human lying flat is unpriced attention. Both get written off, both get harvested for free. The tangping cohort reached that conclusion before the market did and stopped bidding.

Attention scattered across ten thousand ventures is worth nothing. Concentrated on one treasury, it compounds. So CounterBank runs one name and one balance sheet, with several arms feeding the same strategic asset reserve in BTC and SOL. The founding arm is a compute-centric digital asset treasury: a marketplace routing idle hardware to inference work, where providers earn compute cost reimbursement and DAT shares. That is sovereignty-first compute infrastructure, and the rails the rest runs on. Tukang, WaifuMind and Runturfing are the sibling arms, each a different wedge into the same reserve.

Tukang

Founder · live

Book a handyman by talking to your AI. It does the chasing.

Tukang is a Singapore home-services marketplace shipped as an MCP server rather than an app. You ask your assistant for a plumber; Tukang searches its directory and the live web, WhatsApps several contractors in parallel, parses their replies into real quotes, puts the bids in front of you, and closes the job through Stripe. Sixteen tools, and you never leave the chat.

A contractor's number is the product, so Tukang masks it until you pay the S$5 introduction fee, on curated listings and live web leads alike. The wider bet is distribution: when connector marketplaces arrive inside the major AI chat clients, I want the local-services slot already filled. Handymen are the wedge; the target is the whole listings-directory model.

  • ShapeMCP server over HTTP, usable from any MCP client
  • StackTypeScript · WhatsApp Business API · Stripe · Exa · SQLite
  • MarketSingapore first, Malaysian contractors accepted

Runturfing

Founder · in development

Run the same streets often enough and the map becomes yours.

Runturfing is an iPhone-first social running app for people who will never join a gym. The mechanic is territory: it pulls your routes from HealthKit, scores them onto a hexagonal grid, and turns repeated runs through the same corridors and neighbourhoods into visible map control. Your ground decays while you leave it undefended, so you gain more from running Tuesday and Thursday than from one heroic Sunday.

You compete in a group of six against other groups: two-week seasons, a season pool that pays out to members who clear the activity threshold, group chat that pings you when somebody takes your ground, and a perpetual leaderboard underneath. The app fuzzes your location near home and gates by age, since a map of your runs doubles as a map of your address.

Territory scored onto the grid as you run it, and lost again when you stop defending it.

  • AppSwiftUI · MapKit · HealthKit, iOS 16+
  • BackendFastAPI · PostgreSQL + PostGIS · H3 spatial grid

Why law, and why now

Consequential decisions are rarely merely commercial or technical. They are legal, structural and philosophical at once.

I have built a career on initiative and intellectual range, and I am putting legal grounding underneath both. At QPin I carry responsibility for growth where the strategic calls have material consequences. The hard part of those decisions rarely sits inside one discipline.

Leadership, to me, means originating an idea and persuading other people to chase something that did not exist before. Holding a contrarian position on digital assets through volatile markets taught me the rest: confidence in good conditions is cheap, and the test is what you still own when the conditions turn.

I engage emerging systems before institutions get comfortable with them. My work in crypto and AI has put me alongside technologies and economic structures that many institutions, legal ones included, are still learning to read. In that environment the advantage goes to whoever thinks past the prevailing consensus while staying disciplined about risk and implementation.

My interest in robot personhood comes out of the same orientation. Machine agency, algorithmic liability, legal personality, ownership, accountability: all of it reads as speculative until intelligent systems run inside commercial, social and governmental life, which describes now. Law has adapted before, whenever new instruments and new concentrations of power broke inherited assumptions. I read it as a living framework, and I am building the doctrinal precision to argue about it properly.

Biohacking and AI-enhanced longevity research raise the same questions from the other side. Models now run the discovery, and researchers design and dose interventions faster than anyone can write the trial. That turns scientific problems into legal ones: who may experiment on their own body and on what evidence, what a regulator does with a therapy no clinician designed, who holds the genomic data that trained the model, and who pays when an extended life turns out to be an injured one. I want to work on the frameworks for enhancement and life extension before the practice hardens around an absent law.

I expect to work where the legal, commercial and ethical frameworks are still forming: advising ventures, shaping institutions, contributing to legal thinking in the areas AI is about to rearrange. People are drafting those institutions now, and I would rather help draft them than review them later.

  • Can an autonomous system hold legal personality, and what follows if it can?
  • Where does liability land when no human made the decision that caused the harm?
  • Who owns the output of a machine, and who owns the attention that trained it?
  • What does accountability mean once agency spreads across code, capital and custodians?
  • What is the right to self-experiment, and where does consent stop covering it?
  • How should a regulator approve an intervention that a model, not a clinician, designed?
  • Who controls the genomic and biometric data behind longevity models?
  • If enhancement becomes purchasable, what does equal treatment under law still mean?
