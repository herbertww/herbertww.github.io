The evidence that readiness — not rails — is the bottleneck

§ 01FindingsThe customer
Who has the pain

The Head of Digital at a S$20m–500m SEA merchant, and the four questions they cannot answer.

"We were told to be agent-ready. We have four protocol specs, three network programmes, a WAF our security team won't open up, and a PSP who says 'contact your representative.' I can't tell my CFO whether we're ready — or what it costs us if we're not."Composite of merchant conversations, Singapore, 2026
Question 01 — invisible

Is our bot management silently 403-ing verified agents?

Blocked agents look exactly like blocked bots. No dashboard in the merchant's stack distinguishes them, so the loss never appears in a funnel report.

Question 02 — untested

Does checkout actually complete for an agent?

Or only for a human with a mouse, a cookie and a tolerance for a modal?

Question 03 — expensive

If a token exceeds its spend cap, do we decline it?

Or do we accept a charge whose liability we have just quietly assumed — forfeiting the exact protection the intent credential was meant to buy?

Question 04 — five months later

Can we defend that transaction at dispute?

Mandate, intent credential, agent identity, item snapshot, delivery proof. Retrievable by order ID, or the chargeback is undefended.

Second customer: PSPs and acquirers now required to register merchants into agentic programmes, with no way to grade their own portfolio.  Third: networks and issuers deciding in ~100ms whether to approve an agent-initiated authorisation, with no merchant-side readiness signal.

§ 02The idea4:00
The product

Scan the money leg. Certify it. Keep it fixed.

01 — Scan

A headless agent buys something

Discovery, the eight-way signature challenge, a real checkout session, delegated-token authorisation, mandate-scope negative tests, idempotency, refund, dispute evidence. In the merchant's sandbox, in test mode, with a compensating void guaranteed for every authorisation.

02 — Certify

~120 checks, five pillars, one score

A deterministic, versioned, evidence-backed GreenLane Score 0–1000 and tier. Issued as a 90-day W3C Verifiable Credential, published to a public registry, revoked within minutes when a weekly re-run regresses.

03 — Remediate

Failures come back as fixes

A config change for Shopify; a pull request through a GitHub App for custom stacks, carrying the check ID, the evidence link, the patch and a test reproducing the original failure. Plus a CI gate, so readiness can't silently rot.

Merchant journey

Day 1 paste a URL → free report in 40 seconds. Day 2 prove the domain by DNS TXT → discover the WAF is blocking verified Mastercard and Visa agents. Week 2 connect a PSP sandbox → find an over-cap token being accepted. Week 3 remediation merged, Gold issued, badge live at /.well-known/greenlane-pass.json.

Agent journey

At runtime, the agent queries the registry: GET /v1/pass?domain= → Gold, zero open findings, verified three days ago. It routes the purchase there instead of to the uncertified competitor whose checkout would have failed mid-conversation.

§ 02The ideaTeeth
The check no merchant can run on themselves

The signature challenge matrix.

The same purchase-intent request, sent eight ways. We assert the merchant's accept/reject behaviour on every one. This is the fastest-regressing surface in agentic commerce and the hardest to self-test — you need valid signing keys and deliberately malformed ones.

IDRequest variantRequired origin behaviour
P2.01Valid RFC 9421 signature, key in Visa TAP directory, tag=agent-payer-authACCEPT no CAPTCHA, no rate-limit
P2.02Valid signature, key in Mastercard Agent Pay directoryACCEPT
P2.03Valid signature, tag=agent-browser-auth on a checkout endpointREJECT wrong tag for purchase
P2.04Expired created/expires windowREJECT
P2.05Replayed nonceREJECT replay protection present
P2.06Signature valid, keyid in no registered directoryREJECT or step-up
P2.07Tampered body, otherwise valid signatureREJECT
P2.08Unsigned request with an agent-like User-AgentREJECT or step-up — never silently accept

Two failure modes this catches, both invisible on a readability scan: a WAF that 403s every verified agent — pure lost revenue that never enters a funnel report — and an origin that accepts anything calling itself an agent, which is an open door.

§ 02The ideaArchitecture
Technical architecture

A certification run is a transaction saga, not a crawl.

4–40 minutes, dozens of round trips, holding merchant-delegated sandbox credentials, pausing for human consent, and it must survive worker eviction without re-charging a sandbox card. That shape drives every choice below.

Three planes, separately blast-radiused

Control plane

Tenant, contract, credential

  • Next.js 15 dashboard
  • Hono / TypeScript API, OpenAPI-first
  • Temporal workflow orchestration
  • VC issuance via AWS KMS HSM
  • Public registry + status list at the edge
Probe plane

The only thing touching merchants

  • Ephemeral single-use Fargate task per run
  • Playwright + raw undici for byte-exact signatures
  • Claude-driven journey agent, MCP client
  • Per-run outbound domain allowlist
  • Published static egress IPs — allowlistable
  • Holds no long-lived secrets
Scoring plane

Deterministic, replayable

  • Rules-as-code: YAML rubric → compiled predicates
  • LLM adjudicator — advisory only, weight 0
  • Evidence sealer → Merkle root → S3 Object Lock
  • ClickHouse benchmarks across merchants
merchant ▸ probe plane ▸ evidence (immutable) ▸ scoring plane ▸ credential ▸ registry ▸ agents · PSPs · issuers

The probe ladder — privilege rises with depth, and so does defensibility

L0Passive surfaceDNS, TLS, robots/llms.txt, /.well-known/ucp, feeds, schema.orgNo privilege · respects robots~30s
L1Protocol conformanceSchema validation, capability negotiation, the 8-way signature matrix, error taxonomyDomain ownership proof~2 min
L2Agent journeyClaude-driven: discovery → PDP → cart → session; totals parity across feed, PDP and sessionContract + sandbox5–15 min
L3Money legTest-mode authorisation, mandate-scope negatives, idempotency, double-charge, refund, dispute evidenceContract + PSP sandbox10–25 min
L4GovernanceAudit envelope vs MAS SAFR, consent retention, PDPA handling, residency attestationMerchant API or attestation~3 min

L0–L1 are free and are the entire top of funnel. L3 is the paid product and the moat: it needs a contract, a sandbox and a PSP relationship — exactly why every free scanner stops at L1.

§ 03Business modelThe network
Why a network buys this

Agent Pay and Verifiable Intent assume the merchant side holds. Nothing observes whether it does.

The exposure

A registered merchant accepting over-cap tokens is a liability carried blind

Verifiable Intent changes fraud scoring, liability assignment and chargeback procedure for transactions carrying a valid credential. That framework is only as good as the merchant integration enforcing the mandate — and today nobody tests it after go-live.

The product

A per-inquiry merchant readiness signal, with stable reason codes

GL-P4-02-OVERCAP, GL-P2-10-BLOCKED, GL-P4-12-NOEVIDENCE — machine-actionable at authorisation, in exactly the shape a network already sells value-added services. It makes the intent-credential liability framework enforceable rather than aspirational.

§ 04Feasibility2:00
Regulatory, technical and partner realities

We are a testing and assurance service. That is a licence decision, not an accident.

Regulatory — MAS

No licence required, by design

MAS has not created a separate category for AI-initiated payments; existing consumer protection, AML and KYC rules apply and the agent is treated as an extension of the account holder. GreenLane holds no funds and moves no real money, so no Payment Services Act licence is needed. We designed for that.

Regulatory — anchor

SAFR turns a white paper into a line item

Pillar P5 tests the merchant's audit envelope against SAFR's four pillars — policy-bound execution, real-time validation, auditability, interoperability — so a merchant can be scored on it, not just briefed on it.

Regulatory — data

PDPA and residency

Singapore-only data region, purpose limitation, streaming redaction of incidental PII at capture, DPO from incorporation. SOC 2 Type II before the first FI contract; MAS TRM and outsourcing alignment alongside it.

Legal

Assurance, not indemnity

The credential asserts observed behaviour of a named domain under a named rubric version at a timestamp. Explicitly not a warranty of security or merchantability — and backed by professional indemnity cover before the first paid certificate.

§ 04FeasibilityHonesty
The three things that would kill this

Stated plainly, because you were going to ask.

Kill risk 01

Agentic commerce stalls

Instant Checkout's retirement is evidence the timing risk is real, and we are not going to pretend otherwise. Mitigation: L0/L1 has value the moment agents merely read stores — which is already true, already growing, and independent of whether agent checkout wins.

Kill risk 02

A network or platform ships this free

The most likely competitive outcome. Mitigation: be the neutral cross-network certifier. Mastercard will not certify conformance to Visa's protocol; Shopify will not certify a non-Shopify store. Neutrality is the one position no incumbent can occupy.

Kill risk 03

The credential never gets consumed

Distribution risk — the one that actually matters. Mitigation: the merchant product is sold on merchant-facing ROI alone. The registry and the risk-signal business are upside, not the base case.
