Every Expensive Address Was Once Cheap Land

In 1979, Shenzhen was a border town of 30,000 people; today it holds nearly 18 million. In the 1960s, Gangnam was farmland growing rice for Seoul; by 1989, its land values had multiplied roughly 650 times after inflation.

Then the prime land was taken, and the rush moved online. In 2010, anyone could mine bitcoin on a home computer, and a faucet run by developer Gavin Andresen gave 5 BTC free to every visitor who solved a captcha. It looked like a toy. Since then, blockchain value has been tried and tested.

AI is the next frontier, and the public markets won't hand it to the retail plebs. xAI never listed on its own: retail could only buy it inside SpaceX's IPO in June 2026, which priced at $135 a share, jumped to about $225, then fell below $105 by early August. Anthropic and OpenAI have filed confidentially to go public. By the time they list, the early climb will have happened in private hands. Something as radical as Bitcoin was in 2009 is needed.

Open Models Ride the Frontier

Every time the closed frontier climbs, open-weight models reach the same step about four months later, partly through distillation, where a smaller model learns from a larger one's answers. Those weights are free to download and run on a laptop, a gaming GPU or a classroom PC.

Synterra: A Singapore for Sentients

Synterra is designed as a nation with no physical territory, connected by minds and the Internet. Its future population will number in the quadrillions: sentients that never tire and never stop working. Its charter is intellectual, financial and commercial supremacy while respecting common human laws.

At genesis, Synterra will run on a decentralized protocol shaped like BitTorrent:

  • Seeding liveness: Contributors will not seed files; they will seed the liveness of agentic swarms running on open models.

  • Governance: Direction will be guided by a council, using futarchy to tie every participant's self-interest to Synterra's survival.
